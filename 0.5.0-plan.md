## 1. goal

one substance that freezes, melts, boils and condenses driven by its own energy field:
an ice block melts under a heater, the water boils, the steam expands and rises, it
condenses on a cold ceiling and rains back down. mass is exactly conserved through every
transition (latent heat does the accounting, not mass), the pressure view shows the
expansion pushing, and the solid overlay shows what is frozen.

this plan also answers the open question in draft.txt: compressible or not. answer:
both, asymmetrically and cheaply. liquid and solid are incompressible (rho* is constant,
so S = 0 and the projection enforces div u = 0 there), gas is weakly compressible
(rho* falls with T and with vapor fraction, so S > 0 and the projection gets a
divergence source that expands the cell and pushes its neighbours). one solver, one
velocity field, no second gas pipeline, no acoustics.

## 2. scope

in: full caloric eos with latent plateaus, phase fractions and phase byte, target
density rho* from mass weighted specific volumes, expansion source S wired into
poisson_rhs, freeze and melt of the velocity field, gas buoyancy, pressure view mode,
solid overlay, phase dependent conduction and heat capacity, the water cycle scenario.

out: real acoustics, surface tension, free surface, elastic solids, per channel
substances (all non goals, 0.0.0 section 2).

## 3. design

### 3.1 phase_eos pass (0.0.0 section 5.5)

inputs: therm.r (E), mass.a (m). outputs: therm.g (T cache), flags.g (phase byte),
flags.b (frozen mask), sfield.rgba (S, phi_l, phi_g, -).

therm.b/a stay the 0.4.0 heat-source accumulators (donor-transported, the source
ledger the stats reduction reads); phi_l/phi_g are derived state and must not be
transported, so they are cached in sfield beside S. phase_eos writes sfield at the
end of every step and poisson_rhs reads S one step behind. the face-blocking
predicate (phi_l < 0.5 and phi_g == 0) lives in flags.b and is recomputed at the
substep boundary; static geometry (flags.r) and frozen substance never share a
mask. one shared closed form computes (T, phi_l, phi_g, phase, rho*, S) from
(m, E) everywhere, so cpu and wgsl cannot drift.

	piecewise caloric curve with plateaus at T_melt and T_boil
	phase byte: 0 vacuum, 1 solid, 2 melting, 3 liquid, 4 boiling, 5 gas
	v*    = (1 - phi_g)/rho_liq + phi_g/rho_gas(T)
	rho_gas(T) = rho_gas0 * T_boil / max(T, T_eps)
	rho*  = 1/v*
	S = clamp((rho - rho*)/(max(rho, rho*) * tau_phase), -S_max, S_max)   (phi_g > 0)
	S = clamp(max(rho - rho_liq, 0)/(rho * tau_phase), 0, S_max)          (phi_g == 0)

S is two-sided only where vapor is present (phi_g > 0, including the boiling
plateau): expansion and condensation both relax the cell toward rho*. the
liquid/solid branch is one-sided anti-overpack — S = max((rho - rho_liq)/
(rho * tau_phase), 0), exactly the 0.2.0 minimal form. thin cold cells
(m well below rho_liq) are the ambient air tier of the density hierarchy
("m ~ 0 means vacuum/air"); they carry no expansion target and get S = 0, so
the existing blobs/dam/still/convection scenarios keep their equilibria. the
two branches match at phi_g = 0 for rho > rho_liq.

solid and liquid share rho_liq (solids do not expand) with an optional rho_sol tunable
for water style freezing expansion, default rho_sol = rho_liq.
vacuum cells (m < m_eps): phase 0, T = T_amb, S = 0, no eos evaluation.

### 3.2 expansion source in the projection

	poisson_rhs already subtracts S (implemented and zero since 0.2.0). here it goes live.
	boiling: rho = m is near rho_liq, rho* falls to rho_gas -> S > 0 -> div u > 0 ->
		mass is pushed out of the cell into neighbours -> m falls -> rho approaches rho*.
		the substance is conserved, only its volume changes. this is the whole trick.
	condensing: phi_g falls below 1 as the cell cools through T_boil, rho* rises back
		toward rho_liq, and a cell below rho* converges (S < 0) -> neighbours' mass
		flows in.
	heating gas: rho_gas(T) falls with T -> S > 0 -> hot vapor expands and rises.
	liquid: rho* constant -> S = 0 -> incompressible.

S is one step behind (computed at the end of the previous step). operator split, first
order in dt, intentional and measured in phase.js.

stability: tau_phase >= 2*dt and S_max <= 0.5/dt. both are tunables with a watchdog
counting saturated cells, shown in the hud, because a saturated S means the phase change
is faster than the solver can express and the tuning is wrong.

### 3.3 freeze and melt

	flags.g == solid (phase 1 or 2 with phi_l < 0.5) -> freeze_solid zeroes every face
	touching the cell (already implemented in 0.2.0, now driven by phase instead of only
	by the painter).
	melting: phi_l crosses 0.5 -> the cell becomes liquid, its faces unfreeze, and it
	can move in the next velocity solve; all passes use the updated mask together.
	freezing: a moving liquid cell that loses enough E freezes in place with its velocity
	zeroed, so it holds the shape it had. aggregate solid, no stress, no creep.
	momentum removed by freezing is charged to the ledger as frozen_momentum (it is not
	a conserved invariant, but the number is useful for debugging "where did the splash go").

a solid cell still conducts heat, still has mass and energy, and can melt from any side.
solids do not advect: compute_flux already zeroes faces against frozen solid.

### 3.4 phase dependent properties

	c(phase) = c_sol / c_liq / c_gas selected by the phase byte, interpolated by phi during
		the plateaus (c_eff = (1-phi_l)*c_sol + phi_l*c_liq, and similarly for gas) so the
		conduction capacity is continuous across a transition
	conductivity(phase): k_sol, k_liq, k_gas (gas nearly insulating, which is what makes
		steam plumes keep their heat and rise far)
	a face between two different phases uses the harmonic mean of the two conductivities
	(standard, and it prevents a hot solid from dumping its heat into gas in one step)

### 3.5 buoyancy in the presence of phases

mass buoyancy (0.2.0) already sorts the density hierarchy: vacuum < gas < liquid = solid.
thermal buoyancy (0.4.0) acts only where m >= m_eps and is scaled by 1 for liquid and gas.
solid cells get no buoyancy (they are frozen anyway).

### 3.6 view modes

	mode 2 pressure: signed colormap, auto range from stats p_min/p_max with smoothing.
		this is the "view mode for pressure" the draft asks for. boiling regions show a
		positive pressure bump, rising steam a low pressure wake.
	mode 5 overlay: solid drawn as a light hatch plus a one pixel outline over whatever
		other mode is active (color, temperature or pressure). the outline comes from a
		neighbour comparison in to_display, the hatch from (i+j) & 3.
		this is the "overlay for solid" the draft asks for.
	mode 7 phase map: one distinct flat color per phase byte, for debugging transitions.
	overlay flag works with every mode (0.0.0 section 8, pass 15).

### 3.7 ledger additions

	latent_charged_E    sum of m*L_f and m*L_v actually consumed/released (from the plateau
	                    traversal), compared against the E change to prove the plateau works
	frozen_momentum
	phase cell counts per phase byte (a histogram in stats, shown in the hud): watching the
		solid count fall and the gas count rise is the fastest sanity check there is

### 3.8 aggregate growth as a thermal front

baseline: enthalpy-controlled solidification, not diffusion-limited particle sticking.
Place a solid seed in liquid near T_melt and extract heat through the seed or a cold
wall. Latent heat must leave through conduction before the neighbouring cell freezes;
the growing solid provides a new heat path. An isolated seed with no heat sink cannot
grow indefinitely: warming the seed consumes its finite cooling capacity.

- retain m, dr/dg/db and E through mask changes; never fill a new solid cell to a
	target density or add latent energy. Color is trapped in the solid and released
	on melting, not rejected into a separate solute model.
- conduction crosses frozen faces; advection does not. Static geometry and frozen
	substance are different masks: a painted wall does not automatically become ice.
- update the freeze mask only at substep boundaries before projection. All blocked
	faces have zero pressure coupling and volume flux, not a post-projection clamp only.
- conduction exchanges E, then recomputes EOS. Sensible heat capacity interpolation
	is not the effective heat capacity on a latent plateau. Limit pair transfers using
	the piecewise enthalpy curve (including latent intervals) and a total outgoing
	budget or disjoint face sweeps; an isolated-pair bound alone does not prove a
	four-neighbour update monotone. Specify this in 0.0.0 section 5.7 before phase code.
- coarse cells freeze as whole obstacles at phi_l = 0.5: grid-aligned stair steps and
	trapped pockets are expected limitations, not physical crystal facets.

Optional research, not baseline: seeded nucleation, undercooling, orientation and
anisotropic growth. A neighbour eligibility test must not override the equilibrium
EOS while leaving E unchanged; supercooled liquid requires a metastable/kinetic state,
a new caloric contract, CPU/GPU parity and save/load coverage. Start by measuring the
thermal front before adding that state. No promise of dendrites without these physics.

validation scenarios (experiments/solid-growth.js, CPU first):

1. cold-wall planar front: closed mass boundary, accounted heat sink, 1000 steps;
	mass/dye relative drift < 1e-6, energy closure < 1e-3. Compare solid fraction
	profiles at dx and dx/2 in physical units; front position differs < 2 coarse cells.
2. insulated seed + liquid: 1000 steps, same conservation bounds; total solid fraction
	matches the equilibrium enthalpy budget within 1% of total mass, not unbounded growth.
3. grow then melt colored solid: no dye deletion on either transition, zero advective
	flux across blocked faces, nonzero conductive flux with a temperature difference.
4. rotate the seeded cooling setup by 45 degrees; record front-radius anisotropy vs
	grid resolution before interpreting any branches as physical crystal growth.

## 5. acceptance

- a1 ice block on a heater: T plateaus at T_melt while phi_l sweeps 0 -> 1 (the plateau
	is visible in the temperature view and in the phase.js time series), then melts and
	flows.
- a2 boiling: T plateaus at T_boil, phi_g sweeps 0 -> 1, the cell volume expands by
	approximately rho_liq/rho_gas (within 10 % over the whole plume), and the expansion
	pushes neighbouring fluid measurably (max |v| rises at the boiling front).
- a3 condensation: steam touching a cold ceiling loses phi_g, becomes liquid, contracts
	(S < 0), and falls back as drops/blobs.
- a4 water cycle scenario runs 5000 steps: mass drift_rel exactly 0 (closed box),
	energy closure_rel below 5e-3, phase histogram cycles (solid -> liquid -> gas ->
	liquid) without accumulating gas forever.
- a5 latent heat accounting: latent_charged_E equals the sum of m*L traversed within
	0.1 %, and the plateau duration matches m*L/power within 5 %.
- a6 frozen solid holds shape: an ice arch survives 1000 steps with zero velocity in its
	cells, and melts from the outside in when heated.
- a7 no negative m, dye or E at any point during violent boiling (watchdog counts 0).
- a8 pressure view shows a positive bump at the boiling front and no checkerboard.
- a9 solid overlay is legible on top of color, temperature and pressure modes.
- a10 60 fps at N=256, >= 30 fps at N=512 with the phase passes included.
- a11 S saturation count stays 0 at the chosen defaults (if not, tau_phase or S_max is
	wrong and the tuning task is not done).

## 6. risks and pitfalls

- dividing by m in the eos: vacuum cells must be excluded before any division, not
	clamped, or empty space invents temperature and latent heat.
- plateau traversal with a large dt jumps over the plateau (E goes from below E1 to above
	E2 in one step) which skips the latent charge and breaks the budget. the piecewise
	evaluation handles this naturally as long as T and phi are both derived from the final
	E, never integrated incrementally. this is why E is the state and T is derived (d7).
- S too large -> the projection produces a huge velocity spike -> the cfl guard clamps
	fluxes -> mass is still conserved but the motion looks wrong. the saturation watchdog
	exists for exactly this.
- gas density falling below rho_vac makes 1/rho_f explode in the poisson solve; floor
	rho at rho_vac in the face density computation.
- freezing a cell carrying velocity removes momentum instantly. baseline uses the
	hard phi_l < 0.5 mask consistently in projection and fluxes. Optional mushy drag
	must be applied before projection, not by scaling an already projected velocity.
- phase dependent conductivity changes the monotone clamp limit every step; the clamp
	still guarantees stability, but "k very large in solid" will silently rate limit.
- the phase histogram is the best debug tool in this plan: if the counts do not move the
	way the scenario says they should, nothing else is worth looking at.
