# 1.0.0 — floating diagnostic body (buoy) and an optional substep policy

status: implemented (cpu reference, wgsl, app wiring, conformance + headless gpu plumbing checks);
browser visual pass pending
depends on: 0.2.0 (substep rule, scenario), 0.3.0 (dye tracers), gui.1 (control panel)
source brief: draft.txt, "in dam scenario" block

## 1. goal

a rectangle that floats in the dye: it sinks into the blue, is lifted back out, and
settles partially submerged. it must have inertia (no per-frame jumping) and it must not
enter the physics solver. the amount of blue inside the rectangle stands for displaced
volume, so `[r,g,255]` lifts harder than `[r,g,123]` — approximate on purpose.

second, unrelated knob from the same brief: the auto-cfl substep policy becomes optional
and is off by default, so a measurement or a test can pin the sim to one step per frame.

## 2. why it sits outside the solver

the body reads the field; it never writes it. that keeps the conservation claim intact
(no ledger entry, no extra transport, no moving obstacle in the projection) and makes the
whole thing one compute kernel plus a dozen lines of cpu. a body that displaces fluid would
need a per-frame wall mask, re-projection and accounting for the swept volume: a different
plan (see 6, non-goals).

three pieces:

	field sample     one wgsl workgroup (buoy_sample) sums the cell block under the body
	                 and writes six floats into a storage buffer; the cpu twin is
	                 src/core/buoy.js sampleCpu, used by the node tests
	body state       the same module integrates position/velocity from the last sample at
	                 the frame rate; nothing per-substep, nothing per-cell
	display          to_display draws the rectangle as an overlay in every view mode, with
	                 the submerged fraction as a tinted band, so the sampled number is
	                 checkable by eye against the field

the sample buffer is copied to a MAP_READ buffer and consumed in the async readback already
used for stats; the body therefore runs on a sample that is a frame old plus
`buoySampleEvery` frames of staleness. the inertia hides it, and the alternative (a sync
map) would stall the queue.

## 3. model

`+y` is up in the grid, `gravityY` is a positive down magnitude. area `A = w*h` cells.

	sample      blue = Σ mass.b over the footprint, mass = Σ mass.a, cnt, ū = Σu/cnt,
	            v̄ = Σv/cnt, peak = max mass.b inside the footprint
	submersion  sub = clamp(blue/A / peak, 0, 1)      (local peak: independent of how
	            much dye a scenario happens to carry)
	force       a_y = buoyGain * |gravityY| * (blue/A - buoyRho)
	  blue/A > buoyRho  ->  net lift, the body rises; below it the body sinks
	  equilibrium float line is blue/A = buoyRho, i.e. half submerged in a fluid whose
	  dye is 2*buoyRho per cell (the dam block carries b = 0.5*m, so buoyRho 0.25 floats
	  at ~50%)
	drag        (u,v) += (ū - u, v̄ - v) * min(1, dt/buoyDrag)
	  one relaxation toward the mean fluid velocity: couples the body to the flow (it is
	  carried with the water) and damps the heave around the float line
	integrate     v += a_y*dt; drag; clamp |v| to vMax/dt; x += v*dt; inelastic walls
	all quantities are cells and cells/s, like the solver's.

the rectangle is a block of whole cells: its origin is `round(center - size/2)` and the
kernel, the overlay and the sampler all take that from the same params slots, so cpu and
gpu walk identical cells.

## 4. configuration

	substepsAuto     false   auto-cfl policy off = one step per frame (test default);
	                         on = ns = clamp(ceil(maxVel*frameDt/cflTarget),1,substepMax)
	buoyEnabled      1       user toggle; the body still only spawns where a scenario has one
	buoyRefW/H       16,64   reference rectangle at N=512, scaled with the grid, min 4
	buoyU/V          .3,.75  spawn centre as a fraction of N (v is up, so above the dam)
	buoyRho          0.25    float line, in dye mass per cell area
	buoyGain         1        scales lift and weight together (a stronger spring, not a
	                          different float line)
	buoyDrag         0.12 s   velocity relaxation toward the fluid
	buoySampleEvery  4        frames between field samples (and readbacks)

params slots 88..93: buoyX0, buoyY0, buoyW, buoyH, buoySub, buoyShow. keys `u` (policy)
and `y` (body), plus two checkboxes in the control panel.

## 5. validation

- conformance (cpu): the body dropped above the dam dips below the surface and is lifted
  back out; it ends partially submerged (sub in [0.15,0.85]); max |dy| per frame stays
  below one cell; the field after 400 frames is bit-identical to the same run without a
  body. the model alone: brighter blue lifts harder, half a footprint of blue is neutral,
  one frame of full lift moves it ~0.05 cells.
- conformance: the policy branch is covered both ways — off gives ns=1 at any velocity, on
  gives the cfl target then the cap; with the policy off the dam break stays finite and
  non-negative (the outflow limiter, not the substep count, is what guarantees monotone
  transport).
- experiments/buoy.js: float line versus blue level and versus hull density, sample
  refresh interval sweep, grid sizes.
- experiments/gpu-mock.js: mock device drives real app frames — sample requested only on
  cadence frames, buffer copied back at the right size, readback reaches the body state,
  the uniform buffer carries the live rectangle, wgsl indices all resolve to declared
  params slots. plumbing only, not a numeric oracle.

acceptance: in the browser, dam + `n` to it: the body falls in, bobs once or twice and
rides the surface as it sloshes; `0` view shows the tinted hull band; no conservation
meter changes colour with the body on.

## 6. non-goals, and what would come next

no rotation, no displacement of fluid, no two-way coupling, no per-substep sampling, no
buoy in the other scenarios. the lift saturates if a scenario carries more dye than
`buoyRho` per cell area — a body in very blue fluid rides high and can be lifted by spray
and smeared dye; that is the documented approximation, and `buoyRho` is the knob.
a body that displaces fluid needs the moving-mask path (0.2.0 painting plus a swept-area
ledger entry), which is its own plan.
