# 0.9.0 — deferred experiment: lattice boltzmann backend (d2q9 + thermal d2q5)

status: DEFERRED. research, not on the critical path. do not start before 0.5.0 ships.
depends on: 0.1.1 (cpu reference harness), 0.7.0 (backend interface)
user request: "also document for separate deferred experiment Lattice Boltzmann
(D2Q9 + thermal D2Q5)"

## 1. why this exists

the chosen solver (0.0.0 d1) is a mac grid with a pressure projection. lattice boltzmann
(lbm) is the serious alternative and differs in the three places where our model is weakest:

	conservation    lbm streaming is a permutation of populations between neighbours. no
	                interpolation, no upwind smearing, no limiter needed for mass to be
	                exact. our flux advection is exact too, but only because we designed it
	                that way and only for the quantities we route through the flux.
	compressibility lbm is natively weakly compressible: pressure is density (p = cs^2*rho).
	                our gas expansion is a prescribed divergence source with a relaxation
	                time, which has no real pressure response, no waves, no shock.
	cost            one collide+stream pass replaces the whole projection (K=40 jacobi or a
	                multigrid V cycle). for large grids that can dominate everything else.

and it differs where our model is stronger:

	memory          d2q9 needs 9 populations per field set. with thermal d2q5 and three dye
	                tracers we are at roughly 9 + 5 + 15 = 29 floats per cell versus the
	                current ~130 bytes per cell total. f16 populations (what FluidX3D style
	                solvers do) halve it but put rounding pressure on the conservation claim.
	mach limit      meaningful flow speeds must stay below ~0.3 lattice units per step. a
	                game that wants a fast splash hits compressibility artifacts, which is
	                exactly what an incompressible projection never does.
	low viscosity   tau -> 0.5 is unstable. water-like low viscosity at moderate resolution
	                needs tau close to that limit, so we would run artificially viscous.
	rigid solids    bounce-back gives no-slip walls, but "a frozen block that holds its shape"
	                is not native; we would freeze populations, which works but is not a
	                mechanical model.
	debuggability   populations are unintuitive. our current debug views (pressure,
	                divergence, phase) map to lbm only through extra derivation passes.

the user also noted we do not need free surface or surface tension modelling, which removes
the single hardest part of multiphase lbm (interface reconstruction, shan-chen tuning). an
lbm backend here would use the same aggregate trick as the main model: heavy liquid, light
gas, sorted out by gravity, no interface physics. that makes the experiment much smaller
than a general multiphase lbm and is the reason it is worth doing at all.

## 2. hypothesis to test

	an lbm backend on the same aggregate phase model conserves mass and dye better at equal
	or lower cost per step than the projection backend, and gives a usable pressure field for
	free, without breaking the mach limit at game speeds.

## 3. design sketch

	d2q9 populations f_k for momentum, srt/bgk collision with tau from viscosity, guo forcing
	  for gravity and boussinesq buoyancy, halfway bounce-back for static solid and walls,
	  frozen populations for phase solid.
	thermal d2q5 populations g_k for internal energy, same forcing pattern for buoyancy
	  coupling, clamped tau_t for stability, boundary exchange for cooling.
	dye: three options, all measured, pick one
	  a) three d2q5 population sets (sharp, expensive: 15 floats/cell)
	  b) one d2q5 set per dye replaced by conservative flux advection using the lbm velocity
	     (cheap, reuses our 0.3.0 kernel and its exact conservation, slightly softer)
	  c) a single extra population set carrying total dye with the color ratio stored
	     separately (cheapest, worst for sharpness)
	phase: the same caloric eos of 0.0.0 section 5.5 on the energy field. phase changes rho
	  through the equilibrium (rho is a population moment, so "expansion" is native: boiling
	  lowers the local density and the excess mass streams away). no divergence source, no
	  tau_phase, no S_max clamp — that whole machinery disappears.
	units: lattice units with cs^2 = 1/3, dx = 1, dt_lbm chosen so that the mach number of the
	  fastest gameplay velocity stays below 0.2, and a mapping table from our current tunables
	  (gravity in cells/s^2, beta, conductivity) to lattice units. the mapping is the part most
	  likely to waste time; write it first and verify it on a static box.

memory at N=512 (514^2 cells), f32:
	  momentum 9*4*2 (ping pong) = 72 B/cell
	  thermal  5*4*2             = 40 B/cell
	  dye (option a) 15*4*2      = 120 B/cell
	  total ~232 B/cell ~ 61 MB, versus ~34 MB for the current backend.
	f16 populations halve it and must then be re-validated for conservation.

## 4. experiment plan

step 1 cpu only (experiments/lbm-probe.js), N=64 and N=128:
	  - streaming + collision, closed box, uniform rho -> verify mass is exactly constant
	  - guo gravity -> verify a hydrostatic pressure gradient appears (p = cs^2 * rho)
	  - thermal d2q5 with a bottom heater -> verify convection onset, compare onset time and
	    roll count against experiments/convection.js
	  - dye option a and b -> compare front width against experiments/sharpness.js
	  - mach sweep: find the velocity where artifacts appear, in our units
	  - tau sweep: find the low viscosity stability limit, in our units
	  - boiling: drop rho_eq at a hot spot and measure the expansion and the mass conservation
step 2 gpu prototype (experiments/lbm-gpu.html) at N=256 and N=512:
	  - one collide+stream kernel per field set, f32 and f16
	  - per pass timing against the 0.6.0 baseline of the projection backend
	  - conservation drift over 10000 steps, f32 and f16
step 3 side by side scenario (the water cycle of 0.5.0) on both backends:
	  - visual comparison, drift comparison, frame time comparison, memory comparison
step 4 decision, written into this file and into findings-pitfalls-skills.md.

## 5. decision criteria

adopt lbm as an alternative backend (kept alongside, cfg.backend selectable) if all of:
	  d1 mass and dye drift_rel <= the projection backend's, over 10000 steps
	  d2 frame time at N=512 <= 1.2x the projection backend's, or better at N=1024
	  d3 no visible compressibility artifact at the gameplay velocities of the demo
	  d4 the phase model (freeze/melt/boil/condense) reproduces the 0.5.0 acceptance list
	  d5 memory at N=512 <= 100 MB
replace the projection backend entirely only if it also wins on debuggability (the pressure
and divergence views remain available and meaningful) and on robustness across the whole
stability sweep, not just at the defaults.
otherwise: keep this file, keep the cpu probe and its logs as a research artifact, and note
in findings-pitfalls-skills.md when the question should be reopened (answer: if a game needs
real pressure response — explosions, shock, acoustics — or if the projection becomes the
dominant cost at N >= 1024).

## 6. effort

	cpu probe and unit sweeps: one plan worth (0.9.1 if it gets split)
	gpu prototype: one plan worth
	side by side and decision: small
total roughly 2-3 plans, none of which block anything else. the backend interface of 0.7.0
is what makes it cheap to try; without that interface this experiment would require a fork.

## 7. risks

- the mach limit turns out to bind at the speeds the demo wants -> experiment ends at step 1,
	cheaply. that is the most likely outcome and the reason step 1 is cpu only.
- unit mapping errors masquerade as physics bugs. verify hydrostatics before anything else.
- f16 populations break the conservation claim, which removes the main reason to switch.
- frozen-population solids look wrong (a frozen block that slowly relaxes) -> compare against
	the 0.5.0 ice arch acceptance.
- time sink risk: lbm has a large literature and many tempting variants (mrt, trt, cumulant,
	entropic). the experiment uses srt/bgk only, and any variant is a separate deferred plan.
