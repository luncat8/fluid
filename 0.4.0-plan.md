# 0.4.0 — milestone 2: heat, buoyancy, convection

status: planned
depends on: 0.3.0 (E is already transported conservatively)
milestone: draft.txt milestone 2 ("heat map (heat bottom for test) cause heat fluid
and start convection")

## 1. goal

heat a box from the bottom, watch the hot fluid become buoyant, rise, cool at the top
and come back down: steady convection rolls, visible in the temperature colormap, with
an energy budget that closes. temperature becomes a real field with sources, conduction
and buoyancy coupling, and the energy ledger from 0.3.0 starts meaning something.

## 2. scope

in: temperature derivation from E, conduction (clamped pairwise exchange), heater slots,
boundary/wall cooling, boussinesq thermal buoyancy, temperature view mode, energy
budget accounting, viscous dissipation into heat (optional, off by default), probe
dumps for validation.

out: latent heat, melting, boiling, gas expansion (0.5.0). in this plan the caloric eos
runs with L_f = L_v = 0 and phase_mode = none, so every cell is liquid and T = E/(m*c).
that keeps 0.5.0 a constants-and-flags change instead of a rewrite.

## 3. design

### 3.1 temperature

	T(i,j) = E / max(m * c_liq, eps)     for m >= m_eps
	T = T_amb                            for vacuum cells (no capacity, no buoyancy)
written into therm.g by a derive_T pass so every consumer (conduction, buoyancy,
display, stats) reads one cached value instead of recomputing the division.

initial state: T_amb = T_ref = 293 everywhere with substance present, so E is
initialised as m * c_liq * T_amb by the scenario loader. the ledger's initial E uses
the same formula, captured at load.

### 3.2 conduction (clamped pairwise exchange, 0.0.0 section 5.7)

	per face between L and R, both with m >= m_eps:
		dT = T_R - T_L
		C_L = m_L * c_liq, C_R = m_R * c_liq, C_h = (C_L*C_R)/(C_L+C_R)
		dE = conductivity * dT * dt
		limit = 0.5 * C_h * dT
		dE = clamp(dE, -abs(limit), abs(limit)) with the sign of dT
		E_L += dE ; E_R -= dE
	faces against vacuum: dE = 0 (vacuum does not conduct; a tunable gas_conductivity
	arrives with real gas in 0.5.0)
	faces against static solid walls: dE = -h_wall * (T - T_wall) * dt * C, charged to
	boundary_loss_E (default h_wall = 0, insulating)

the exchange must be ping pong, never in place: both cells have to read the same
pre-update E so that the two writes are exact opposites. in place would let the second
cell see the first cell's update and silently destroy conservation. this is the single
most important implementation detail of this plan.

### 3.3 heat sources

	heater slots reuse the emitter table (kind = heat): dE += power * weight * dt,
		weight from the same uploaded falloff table as the dye emitters so the ledger
		matches the gpu exactly. injected_E += the same amount.
	domain edge cooling (per edge flag): dE -= h_edge * (T - T_amb) * C * dt, charged to
		boundary_loss_E. the top edge is the usual cold plate for convection tests.
	mouse heating: pointer with kind = heat paints hot fluid while dragging (the
	"heat bottom for test" case can also be done by hand).

### 3.4 buoyancy

	a_heat = -g_vec * beta * (T - T_ref)
added in the forces pass alongside the mass buoyancy term of 0.2.0. beta default 4e-4
per K, tuned in t9 so a 40 K difference produces visible motion within a second at
N=256 without making the whole field jittery.

the mass buoyancy term and the thermal term coexist: heavy cold water sinks because of
both, hot water rises because the thermal term wins. this is the boussinesq shortcut
(decision d8): density in the projection stays m based, so no tiny density differences
have to be resolved by the poisson solve.

### 3.5 viscous dissipation (optional, default off)

the clamped momentum exchange of viscosity conserves momentum but not kinetic energy.
when viscosity > 0, the lost kinetic energy per face is added to the internal energy of
the two cells (half each) and charged to the ledger as dissipated. off by default
because viscosity is 0 by default; implemented so the energy budget stays closed if a
game turns viscosity on.

### 3.6 energy budget

	injected_E (heaters, emitters)
	boundary_loss_E (edge and wall cooling, open edge outflow carries E with it)
	dissipated_E (viscous, and later phase work)
	stored_E = sum(E)
	kinetic = sum(0.5 * rho * |u|^2) over cells, reconstructed from face velocities
closure = (initial_E + injected_E - boundary_loss_E + dissipated_E) - (stored_E + kinetic)
closure_rel = closure / max(injected_E, initial_E, eps)
hud shows closure_rel with the same coloring as drift_rel. acceptance is closure_rel
below 1e-3 over 1000 steps of the convection scenario.

### 3.7 view modes and probes

	mode 1 temperature colormap: blue (T_min) -> cyan -> green -> yellow -> red (T_max),
		range either fixed from params or auto-fit from the stats min/max with smoothing
	mode 6 thermal debug: T - T_ref signed, so rising and falling currents are obvious
	overlay of velocity arrows in the temperature view (toggle) to see the rolls
	key p dumps a vertical profile (T, v, m at a chosen column) to the console as csv
	key y dumps a horizontal profile. used by convection.js parity and by hand tuning.
	hud adds: T_min, T_max, mean T, max |v|, and a small ring buffer sparkline of the
		last 200 samples of (mean T, max |v|) drawn into a 2d canvas overlay (cheap,
		off by default, toggle with key w). no per frame allocation: fixed ring buffers.

### 3.8 pass graph changes

	insert after advect_scalar / limit_flux:
		derive_T      (therm.r, mass.a -> therm.g)
		conduction    (therm -> therm', ping pong)
		heat_sources  (therm' -> therm, in place is fine here: sources are local and
		                 accounted, no pairwise coupling)
		derive_T again (cheap, keeps therm.g consistent after the sources)
	forces now reads therm.g for the buoyancy term (it already reads mass.a).

## 5. acceptance

- a1 bottom heated box: visible plumes within the onset time measured by convection.js
	(cpu) plus 20 %, and a steady roll pattern after that.
- a2 hot fluid rises, cold fluid sinks: max |v| is upward in hot regions (sign check in
	the convection report), and the mean temperature profile becomes stratified with a
	mixed layer.
- a3 energy closure_rel below 1e-3 over 1000 steps, below 5e-3 over 5000 steps.
- a4 conserved quantities still exact: m and dye drift_rel 0 in a closed box with
	heaters on (heaters change E only, never mass).
- a5 E drift_rel below 1e-6 with conduction on and heaters off (conduction is pairwise
	conservative; any drift here means an in place update bug).
- a6 conduction never overshoots: no cell's T crosses a neighbour's T in one step
	(monotone exchange), verified by the cpu monotone test and a gpu min/max watchdog.
- a7 no NaN or Inf at any point with beta and conductivity at their defaults and at
	10x their defaults (stability sweep recorded in stability.js).
- a8 60 fps at N=256 and >= 30 fps at N=512 with the thermal passes included.
- a9 temperature view reads as a heatmap: the hot bottom and the cold top are obvious
	without tuning, and the auto range does not flicker.

## 6. risks and pitfalls

- in place conduction breaks energy conservation silently (see 3.2). ping pong.
- vacuum cells have C = 0: any division by C must be avoided by the m >= m_eps guard,
	not by clamping C to eps (that invents energy).
- buoyancy applied to vacuum cells creates fast invisible jets; guard with the same
	m >= m_eps test.
- explicit conduction is dt limited; the clamp makes it stable but "too conductive"
	silently becomes rate limited. if the convection looks wrong, check whether the clamp
	is active (count clamped faces in stats) before blaming buoyancy.
- T_ref and T_amb must be the same constant or a uniform field starts moving by itself.
- auto range colormaps that use per frame min/max flicker; smooth with a slow filter
	and keep a fixed range mode for tests.
- energy carried out by an open edge must be charged to boundary_loss_E with the same
	upwind value the transport used, or the closure looks broken.
- the kinetic energy reconstruction from mac face velocities has a half cell ambiguity;
	use the standard average of the two adjacent faces and document it, because closure
	depends on it being consistent between cpu and gpu.
