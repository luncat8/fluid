matched in the first two. The quality verdict therefore comes from the oracle,
and the hardware numbers are used for time only.

## what the oracle said

Two protocols, because they answer different questions:

- `experiments/mg-samestate.js` (controlled): every candidate warms the same
  180 (90 at N=512) steps at the pre-t11 reference (2 cycles, pre4/post8,
  coarse K=60), then runs exactly one measured step. This is solver quality.
- `experiments/mg-defaults.js` (trajectory): each row warms up on its own
  setting and runs a 30-90 step window. This is "what a long run under this
  setting looks like"; its peak metric follows where the chaotic splash is.

Pinned dt=1/240 in both. `rel` = divAfter/divBefore (the a2 number), `true` =
`mg.residualNorm()/divBefore` (the 4.1 judge, operator units). Note `rel` is
only roughly dt*true: the two maxima can land on different cells (dam N=512:
rel 5.5e-3 vs dt*true 2.3e-4).

What it says:

- K=20 costs +16 % peak divergence on the liquid cases and 8 % at N=512 in the
  controlled row, while the true Poisson residual moves by +0.01 % (dam) and
  +0.07 % (blobs); it is free in boiling flow. The trajectory rows amplify that
  to +69 % (dam N=128) and +193 % (dam N=256) because the flow itself evolves
  differently — that is the honest range, not one number.
- K=40 costs +3.6-6.3 % divergence in the controlled row and keeps the
  trajectory ratio below the a2 1e-3 bar at N=64/128/256 (2.5e-4/4.6e-4/8.8e-4),
  where K=20 crosses it at N=256 (1.9e-3). No hardware timing row exists for
  K=40, so it is recorded as the conservative fallback, not adopted.
- 1 cycle costs 21-22x peak divergence on dam/blobs at N=128 (rel 7.7e-3 and
  4.3e-3, above the 1e-3 bar; blobs' true residual +11 %), but only +23 % at
  N=512 during the early splash and nothing in jet/cycle. The damage is phase-
  and grid-dependent, which is exactly why it cannot be a default: the worst
  case, not the average, is what a default has to survive.

The a2 "rel < 1e-3" bar is a settled-state target: at N=512 during the initial
splash even the reference reads 5.5e-3, while dam at N=128 after 180 warmup
reads 3.7e-4. The K=20 default meets it at N=64/128 (3.1e-4/7.7e-4) and misses
it at N=256 (1.9e-3) in the trajectory rows; the K=40 fallback meets it at all
three. This is recorded rather than hidden: the adopted row is the one with a
measured hardware before/after (a6), and the a2 trade is one URL away from
being reversed.

Process note: the first mg-defaults capture was discarded. It had been started
before the solver split landed in the working tree, so its script's
`mgCoarseIters` overrides were read by code that still used `pressureIters`
and its "K20" rows were actually K60 rows (they matched the baseline to three
digits). Always start a capture after the code under test is final, or check
that the candidate rows actually differ.

## decision

- ADOPTED: coarse K 60 -> 20 at 2 cycles. Controlled cost +16 % peak
  divergence (+8 % at N=512) with the true Poisson residual unchanged and no
  cost in boiling flow; hardware 25-48 % faster (press 2.50/1.73/1.64/1.71 ->
  1.83/1.19/1.13/1.17 ms/substep, fps 49/45/43/33 -> 72/67/71/49 for
  blobs/dam/cycle/jet).
- REJECTED as a global default: 1 cycle. Residual-neutral in boiling flow but
  21-22x worse peak divergence on the liquid cases (rel above the 1e-3 bar),
  and the hardware run points the same way (blobs' sampled maxDiv 4.67 vs 3.16
  at equal dt, cycle's sampled maxM 1.476 vs 1.017).
- FALLBACK, recorded not adopted: K=40. Within 6 % controlled divergence and
  below the a2 bar at every measured resolution, at roughly half the dispatch
  saving. It needs a hardware capture before it can replace K=20 (a6).
- OPEN: a scenario-conditional 1 cycle for boiling flow (jet/cycle), where it
  is free at 45 % of the projection work. Needs the fixed-policy hardware A/B.

## changes

- `src/core/config.js`: new default `mgCoarseIters: 20`; `pressureIters: 60`
  stays the red-black / mg=0 sweep count. `solverIters(cfg)` and
  `setSolverIters(cfg, v)` are the only places that pick the active key, so one
  shared number cannot silently mean 16x different work on the two paths. A
  hand-built cfg that only sets `pressureIters` keeps the old single-knob
  meaning. `writeParams` mirrors the active value.
- `src/core/solver.js`, `src/gpu/solver.js`: the solve reads `solverIters(cfg)`.
- `src/app/{main,input,controls,hud}.js`: `?k=` and the slider/`+`/`-` keys
  drive the active solver's count; the HUD prints `K=20 coarse` under
  multigrid and `K=60` for the fallback.
- `experiments/browser-tests.html`: `&k=` uses the active solver; the header
  and every scenario row print `K=20 coarse` next to `solver=MG`, so a saved
  report is self-describing.
- `experiments/mg-work.js`, `mg-work2.js`, `multigrid.js`, `gpu-parity.mjs`:
  the MG rows name `mgCoarseIters` so the logged studies still reproduce.
- `experiments/mg-samestate.js` (new): the controlled one-step comparison, with
  the K=40 ladder row.
- `experiments/mg-defaults.js` (new): multi-step rows for the same configs,
  `--n512` for the target-resolution dam row; both scripts document which
  question they answer.
- `experiments/conformance.js`: the dam divergence bar is pinned to the coarse
  K=60 it was recorded at (its 64^2 trajectory moved, see above); it is not
  loosened.
- `experiments/solid-growth.js`: pins its two MG rows to coarse K=8, the
  setting their ledger tolerance was recorded at.

## validation

- `node experiments/run-all.js --quick`: 11/11 suites.
- `node experiments/run-all.js`: 17/17 suites. Conformance and solid-growth
  failed against the new default until their bars were pinned to the settings
  they were recorded at; both pass afterwards.
- the oracle logs quoted above are the tracked rows; the three hardware
  reports are unchanged inputs.
- no profiler or conservation path was touched; every scenario in the three
  browser runs stayed OK with drift <= 3.3e-6 and no negative cells.
