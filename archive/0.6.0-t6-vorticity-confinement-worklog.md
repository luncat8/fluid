## what changed

- `src/core/config.js`: `vorticityConfinement: 0`. eps in 1/s, 0 is off. cpu
  only, like `advectMac`: the gpu path does not read it, so a flag the wgsl does
  not honour would be a lie.
- `src/core/solver.js`: the force. `F = eps*h*(N x omega)`, N the unit gradient
  of |omega|, omega the z component of the curl at the cell centre. Three
  details decide whether the row means anything:
  - the curl is read with a free-slip wall: a blocked face mirrors the opposite
    interior face, so the wall's zero velocity is not read as a resolved shear.
    Masking the wall instead would put a huge |omega| spike into the gradient
    and push the flow at the boundary.
  - the cell centred force is averaged onto the faces: the u face (x,y) sits
    between the cells (x-1,y) and (x,y), the v face (x,y) between the cells
    (x,y-1) and (x,y). The force goes into the existing force pass, before the
    clamp, so it is projected like every other force.
  - the scratch (omega, |omega| and the force) is allocated on first use, so the
    shipping path with eps=0 does not pay for it and the advection rows are
    untouched.
- `diag.velClamp` counts the absolute velocity clamp. The confinement force is
  an energy source, so the watchdog is not "no new extremum" (that is the
  advection schemes' rule) but "the velocity never reaches the clamp".
- `experiments/vortex-decay.js`: the confinement rows. eps=2 and eps=8 by
  default (`--eps 0,1,4` overrides), a force slice timed next to the advection
  slice, and a decision block that reads the vorticity and the enstrophy, not
  the energy: the force adds energy on purpose, so the retained-KE row stops
  being a diffusion metric for these rows.

## measurement (N=128, 960 steps = 4 s, the shipping projection)

| | sl | sl+conf eps=2 | sl+conf eps=8 | sl+conf eps=32 (rejected) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| peak vorticity at 4 s | 2.710 | 3.008 (1.11x) | 9.309 (3.44x) | 44.8 (16.5x) |
| enstrophy at 4 s | 1.000x | 1.029x | 1.843x | 33.0x |
| retained KE at 4 s | 0.5628 | 0.5578 (0.99x) | 0.5095 (0.91x) | 0.3160 (0.56x) |
| maxDiv at 4 s | 5.04e-3 | 5.04e-3 | 8.24e-3 | 1.76e-1 |
| peak speed at 4 s | 53.74 | 53.99 | 52.82 | 55.55 |
| velocity clamp | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| cpu ms/step (force slice) | 41.20 (3.04) | 44.78 (4.53) | 46.06 (5.42) | 45.91 (5.49) |

- eps=2 is nearly free: 1.11x the peak vorticity, the divergence unchanged to
  the printed digits. It is the setting that could ship.
- eps=8 is the sharp one: 3.44x the peak vorticity and 1.84x the enstrophy at
  4 s, for 1.6x the residual divergence and 0.91x the retained energy. The
  trade is visible in the numbers: the force concentrates the rotation into a
  smaller core, so the vortex is sharper and the ring around it is weaker.
  That is the "soft vortices" complaint answered, and it is also a cost — the
  energy that leaves the ring does not come back.
- eps=32 is where the force stops being free: the enstrophy is 33x and the
  residual divergence is 35x the shipping row. Rejected: a force that grows the
  divergence that fast is a noise amplifier in the product, where the boiling
  flow already spikes.
- cost: the force pass is two extra passes over the velocity field. The force
  slice goes 3.04 -> 4.53 ms (eps=2) -> 5.42 ms (eps=8) per step at N=128, and
  the whole step 41.2 -> 44.8 -> 46.1 ms; the step grows by more than the slice
  because the projection also gets a sharper field to solve. On the device it
  is two more dispatches and one more rgba32float scratch (omega, |omega| and
  the force), against a vel group that is 2 % of the substep, so the frame cost
  is small but not zero.

## decision

Not adopted, and the flag stays 0. The quality half is measured and it is
real: at eps=8 the stirred vortex keeps 3.44x the peak vorticity of the
shipping scheme. The cost half is a device number and there is no device row
and no wgsl pass (t17), so there is nothing to adopt yet. The same rule as t5:
measure the scheme on the cpu, take the device row before it ships.

What is worth keeping from this: eps=2 is free enough to be the default
candidate for a game build, and the free-slip wall convention is the part of
the implementation that a port has to reproduce exactly, because a masked wall
silently turns the force into a boundary push.

## validation

- The shipping path is unchanged: `node experiments/residual-spike.js` still
  reproduces `2026-10-21-residual-spike.log` to the printed digits (weight jump
  1.000e+3 -> 4.995e+5, melt 0 -> 2.000e+5, dam N=64 worst end/solve 5.56e+2 at
  step 10, solve/rhs 1.356e-4), and the sl and mac rows of the new log match
  the retained `2026-10-21-vortex-decay.log` digit for digit.
- The oracle asserts the invariants it can assert: the velocity clamp watchdog
  stays 0 on every row, and the advection rows still hold "no new extremum"
  (the confinement rows are exempt by name, because their extremum is the
  point).
- `node experiments/run-all.js`: 20/20 in 1039 s, with the two confinement rows
  in the suite (vortex-decay.js 227 s of it).

## next

t17 is the wgsl pass and the device row: the curl, the force in one rgba32float
scratch, and the force folded into the existing forces dispatch. It has to
reproduce the free-slip wall convention and the eps=2/eps=8 numbers above, or
it is not the same force.

t16 (the mac-cormack port) and t17 both need a device. The cheapest open items
that do not are t8 (pass fusion: derive_T into conduction, heat_sources into
phase_eos) and t13 (the readback ring and the display-size trial). t8 is a
kernel change too, so its adoption also waits on a device row; what can be
done without one is to write the fused kernel and check it against the
unfused one on the cpu oracle.
