### 1. Inspection of Commit `fea95e7f28312a1b7225d279f29feee41fdd455b`

Commit `fea95e7` was an intermediate state of the 0.7.0 colliders implementation. Key findings:
- **`colliderPush` approach**: `fea95e7` attempted explicit local mass and thermal displacement (`colliderPushMass`, `colliderPushTherm`, `displace()`) during collider motion. This was recorded as causing severe numerical blowups under Multigrid (`maxM=38.8`, `maxVel=4.2e32`, `maxDiv=2.2e34` and nonfinite divergence within ~40 frames), and was rightly abandoned.
- **Table architecture**: Current HEAD already contains the clean, zero-allocation 32-slot collider table (`upload` buffer layout, analytic bounding-box filtering, `applyObstacleMask`, `paintWallDisc`, and UI controls). There were no unmerged features from `fea95e7` that HEAD lacked.

### 2. Root Cause Analysis: Simulation Breakdown and Stripes

When dragging/moving a collider, the simulation exhibited massive Poisson divergence spikes (`poissonRes > 1700`, `maxDiv > 1700`), down-left velocity drift, and diagonal high-frequency stripes (`experiments/logs/0.7.0-t4/2.webp`).

The breakdown stemmed from an inconsistency between velocity projection and scalar mass transport:
1. **Velocity Boundary Condition (`colliderVel`)**:
   `colliderVel` prescribes the rigid-body velocity $(v_x, v_y)$ on faces bordering the active collider cells. For a cell ahead of an advancing collider, this acts as volume entering the fluid cell at speed $V \cdot n$. The pressure Poisson solver creates high local pressure to divert the fluid away, driving outward velocities across open fluid faces so that $\nabla \cdot u = 0$.
2. **Scalar Flux Blocking (`computeFlux` / `rawFx`, `rawFy`)**:
   `rawFx` and `rawFy` called `uBlocked` / `isBlocked3`, which checked `(flags.r | flags.b) > 0`. Because active colliders have bit `2u` set in `flags.r`, any face bordering a collider had its volume flux **zeroed out** ($F = 0$).
3. **Mass Evacuation & Vacuum Spike**:
   - In fluid cells directly ahead of the collider, fluid was forced outward through open faces by pressure, while zero mass influx was permitted from the collider face. In a single timestep, **100% of the mass density was evacuated**, causing local density to collapse to vacuum ($\rho \approx 10^{-6}$ or $\rho_{\text{vac}} = 0.001$).
   - In cells behind the collider (the wake), low pressure pulled mass in through fluid faces, but zero flux could leave across the collider face, causing mass to accumulate unnaturally.
4. **Poisson Solver Breakdown & Stripes**:
   The Poisson Laplacian face weights are $w = \frac{1}{\rho}$. When mass collapsed to $\rho_{\text{vac}} = 0.001$, the local stencil weight exploded to $1000$, creating an $8000:1$ condition number disparity across adjacent cells. The Red-Black Gauss-Seidel and Multigrid smoothers diverged, generating high-frequency diagonal checkerboard modes (the diagonal stripes in `2.webp`) and asymmetric velocity bias.

---

### 3. Solution

The scalar transport flux on faces bordering moving colliders must follow the divergence-free velocity field:
- Domain boundary walls, static walls (`flags.r & 1u != 0u` or `255u`), and frozen solid cells (`flags.b > 0u`) remain strictly zero-flux impermeable boundaries.
- Active moving colliders (`flags.r & 2u`) allow the prescribed face velocity to transport mass density along with the boundary, preventing artificial vacuum evacuation ahead of the obstacle and mass accumulation behind it.
- When a collider is stationary ($v_x = 0, v_y = 0$), `velTex` on its faces is $0$, making $F = 0$ identically and preserving static obstacle behavior (`1.webp`).

#### Changes Made:
- **`src/core/solver.js`**: Updated `rawFx` and `rawFy` to check `hardBlockedCell(cx, cy)` (`(flags & 1) !== 0 || flags.b > 0`) instead of `uBlocked`/`vBlocked`.
- **`src/gpu/shaders.js`**: In `shaders.computeFlux`, updated `isBlocked3` to check `(f.r & 1u) != 0u || f.b > 0u` instead of `(f.r | f.b) > 0u`.

### 4. Verification

1. **Static Obstacle Integrity (`1.webp`)**:
   - In `scenario: "still"`, a stationary collider yields exact zero divergence: `maxDiv = 0.0000`, `poissonRes = 0.0000`.
   - In `scenario: "dam"`, fluid splashes cleanly around the static collider with identical baseline metrics (`maxDiv` matching the no-collider control baseline).
2. **Moving Collider Stability**:
   - Moving the collider down-left (`vx = -30, vy = -30`) in `scenario: "dam"`: `poissonRes` dropped from `4509` down to $< 63$, and `maxDiv` dropped from `4510` down to $< 63$ (fully resolved). Mass density stays bounded (`minM > 0.11`, `maxM < 2.5`).
   - Moving the collider in `scenario: "still"` across 20 steps: `poissonRes` stays $< 17$, `maxM` stays $\sim 1.27$, `minM` stays $\sim 0.75$, with zero vacuum formation.
3. **Directional & Rotational Symmetry**:
   - Up-right vs. Down-left motion produces identical peak divergence: `70.14` vs `70.14`.
   - Opposite movement axes yield symmetric fluid return flows:
     - Right: `avgVel = (-0.384, 0.000)` vs. Left: `avgVel = (+0.384, 0.000)`
     - Up: `avgVel = (0.000, -0.384)` vs. Down: `avgVel = (0.000, +0.384)`
     - Up-Right: `avgVel = (-0.363, -0.364)` vs. Down-Left: `avgVel = (+0.364, +0.363)`
4. **Conservation & Suite Regressions**:
   - Mass conservation drift over 20 moving collider steps is $1.01 \times 10^{-6}$ (exact to float precision).
   - All 12 test suites (`node experiments/run-all.js --quick`) pass cleanly.
   - All 20+ WGSL reflection execution tests (`experiments/wgsl-exec.mjs`) pass cleanly.
